# Custom VTOL RC Plane — Design and Build Guide (v0.2)

Every part is generated by the scripts in `scripts/`; `scripts/params.py` is the single source
of truth for dimensions. Numbers below come from `scripts/mass_balance.py` (masses from the
sliced G-code) and are checked by `scripts/check_parts.py`. If this file and the scripts ever
disagree, the scripts win.

## Configuration

- **Layout:** tilt-rotor tricopter, the same architecture as the Flightory Stallion: two front
  motors on tilting yokes ahead of the wing, one fixed lift motor on the tail boom, V-tail.
  Yaw in hover is differential tilt of the front motors.
- **Flight controller:** Pixhawk 6C, PX4 tilt-rotor VTOL (`PX4_GUIDE.md`). ArduPilot is the
  fallback; the hardware is the same.
- **Manual link:** ELRS receiver. Phone-over-4G MAVLink can be added later.

## Coordinates used in this guide

x is measured aft from the nose tip, y toward the right wing tip, z up from the fuselage
centreline. The wing's flat bottom is at z = −5 mm. The left side is the mirror image of the
right; every left-hand part has its own file.

## How it is built

- **Skins (LW-PLA, vase mode):** wing sections, ailerons, fuselage sections, V-tail panels and
  ruddervators are single-wall shells, open at both ends. Vase mode cannot print holes, so
  every hole in a skin is drilled by hand (table below).
- **Structure (PETG):** every skin end slides 10 mm over a PETG spigot ring (wing ribs, boom
  cup, couplers, V-tail ribs). The rings are 0.85 mm inside the skin surface: 0.6 mm for the
  foamed wall plus 0.25 mm slip.
- **Wing:** two carbon spars per panel run from the root rib (y = 60) to the tip rib (y = 710):
  main 10×8 mm at x = 432, z = 3.5; rear 6×4 mm at x = 480, z = 1. The panels plug onto
  joiners that cross the fuselage in two printed frames: an 8×6 mm tube 420 mm long inside
  the main spars, and a 4 mm rod 320 mm long inside the rear spars. An M2.5 nylon pin locks
  each rear spar to the rear joiner.
- **Tilt mechanism:** a 6 mm boom rib sits between the root and mid sections at y = ±280. Its
  tongue runs forward into a PETG pod that holds the tilt servo. The yoke carries the motor
  and straddles the pod. Its inboard arm is screwed to the servo horn (direct drive). Its
  outboard arm turns on a flanged bearing on the pod's axle boss. The tilt axis is at x = 315,
  65 mm ahead of the LE. Tilt range is −20° (back) to 90° (forward); 0° is thrust up.
- **Fuselage:** four sections joined by PETG couplers at x = 240, 370 and 610. The nose
  (`fuse_1`) comes off for battery access. A 16×14 mm carbon tail boom runs from x = 420 to
  1050 at z = −20. It passes through both frames, the boom web in `coupler_610` and the
  `pod_collar`. The rear motor mount clamps the boom at x = 820. The tail block on the end of
  the boom carries the V-tail.

## Before printing the full set

1. **Measure the tilt servos.** The pod bay fits a 24 × 13 mm body, 21 mm deep. The tab holes
   are 28 mm apart, the output spline is 6 mm from the front of the body, and the tab face is
   ~11 mm below the top of the spline. If yours differ, change `SERVO_*` in `params.py` and run
   `build_all.py` before printing the pods and yokes.
2. **LW-PLA test:** print `wing_tip_r` on the S1. It should weigh 14.7 g ±15 % and survive a
   gentle flex. If not, change the flow in `filament_lwpla.json` and re-slice. Every vase part
   prints at 17–30 mm/s, so one calibration covers them all.
3. **Fit test:** print `rib_tip_r` and slide its spigot into that tip skin. It should go in with
   light hand force. If it is tight, sand the ring; never force a skin.
4. Then print the rest (`tracker.md`). Nothing needs supports.

## Assembly

Dry-fit every step before mixing glue. Use epoxy for every carbon bond (sand the carbon first)
and every structural PETG joint. Thin epoxy or medium CA is fine for skin-to-spigot joints.

### 1. Wing panels (each side)

1. Ream the spar holes in every rib and the boom cup to 10.25 mm / 6.25 mm with a hand-held
   drill bit, until both spars slide through all of them.
2. Epoxy the `boom_cup` to the inboard face of the `boom_rib`. Thread both spars (wrapped in
   tape, so they do not get glued) through the cup and the rib to align them. The cup's plate
   closes the rib's wire groove.
3. Aileron servos: fit each servo into a `servo_cage`. Cut a hatch in the bottom skin of
   `wing_mid` (around y ≈ 320) and `wing_tip` (around y ≈ 530), between the spars
   (x ≈ 437–475). Glue the cage to the inside of the bottom skin with the servo arm coming out
   through a slot. Run each lead to the wire hole at x = 457.
4. Thread the panel onto the spars from the root: `rib_root`, `wing_root`, boom rib with
   cup, `wing_mid`, `rib_midtip`, `wing_tip`, `rib_tip`. Feed the aileron leads (and, on the
   right wing, the pitot tubing) through the Ø10–12 holes at x = 457 in every rib. Epoxy
   the spars into the rib bosses and the skins onto the spigots. Keep the panel flat on the
   table and sight along the LE for twist before the glue sets.
5. Ailerons: tape hinge along the top surface. They deflect ±25°: the bevel under the
   aileron's front face clears the wing. Glue a `control_horn` under each aileron in line
   with its servo arm. Fit a 1.2 mm pushrod with a Z-bend.
6. Lock pin: drill Ø2.5 straight down through the wing at x = 480, y = ±68, using the root
   rib's vertical guide hole. It goes through the skin, the rib ring and the rear spar. The rear
   joiner gets the matching hole at final assembly.

### 2. Tilt mechanism (each side)

1. Press an F693ZZ flanged bearing into the yoke's outboard arm. Melt an M3 heat-set insert
   into the pod's axle boss.
2. **Screw the motor onto the yoke now**: 4× M3×8. Once the yoke is on the pod, the screw
   heads can't be reached. Check by hand that no screw touches the windings.
3. Epoxy the pod onto the boom-rib tongue and bolt it with 2× M3×35 + nyloc nuts (the pod is
   28 mm wide). The 6 mm tongue goes into a 6.3 mm slot; if the first-layer squish makes it
   tight, sand the tongue. The motor and servo wires run in the groove under the tongue,
   through the cup into the root section.
4. Fit the tilt servo in the pod bay; its tabs screw to the pod's inboard face. Power the servo
   at its centre position and set the yoke to 35° (the middle of −20…90°). Then drill the
   yoke's inboard arm to match the servo horn and screw the horn on.
5. Slide the yoke over the pod with the horn on the servo spline. On the outboard side, fit the
   **0.5 mm shim** (OD ≤ 5 mm) between the bearing's inner race and the axle boss, then the
   M3×12 screw and washer. The shim fills the 0.5 mm running gap. Without it, tightening the
   screw pulls the yoke against the pod and it binds. The yoke must swing freely from −20°
   to 90° with the screw tight.
6. Leave a loop in the motor wires at the pod's outboard wire hole so they flex through the full
   tilt range.

### 3. Fuselage and tail boom

1. Ream every boom hole to 16.3 mm while the parts are still loose: `frame_main`, `frame_rear`,
   the web of `coupler_610`, `pod_collar`, `rear_motor_mount` and `tail_block`. The boom must
   slide through each one by hand.
2. Mark and drill both sides of `fuse_3`: main joiner Ø8.5 at x = 432, z = +3.5; rear joiner
   Ø4.5 at x = 480, z = +1; wing wiring Ø12 at x = 457, z = +7.
3. Slide `frame_main` and `frame_rear` into `fuse_3`. Push the joiners through the skin holes
   and the frame sleeves to align them, then epoxy the frames to the skin.
4. Epoxy `coupler_370` between `fuse_2` and `fuse_3`. Epoxy `coupler_610` into the aft end of
   `fuse_3` with its boom web forward, inside `fuse_3`.
5. Prepare the boom. Drill Ø6 at x ≈ 795 for the rear motor leads, on the side that will face
   up. Dry-fit the `tail_block` on the aft end, square to that hole, and mark the boom through
   the block's two wire holes (x ≈ 1001 right, 1024 left). Take the block off and drill Ø6 there.
6. Slide the boom into `fuse_3` through its open aft end, front end first. It passes the
   `coupler_610` web, `frame_rear` and `frame_main`; stop when its front end is at x = 420,
   with the lead hole facing up. Epoxy it at all three, reaching in from the open aft end.
7. Epoxy the `pod_collar` into the tail end of `fuse_4`. Slide `fuse_4` over the boom's aft end,
   through the collar and onto the aft half of `coupler_610`. Epoxy it to the coupler and the
   collar to the boom. Nothing aft of x ≈ 450 can be reached afterwards, and nothing lives in
   `fuse_4`. The boom's aft end stays free for the motor mount and tail block.
8. Melt the M3 insert into `coupler_240` and epoxy the coupler 20 mm into the front of
   `fuse_2`. Epoxy in the `fc_shelf` and the `battery_tray`.
9. Slide on the nose. Drill Ø3.4 through its bottom centreline at x = 228 (12 mm ahead of its
   aft edge) into the insert, and fit the M3×12 nylon screw with a 12 mm nylon washer. The
   nose tip has a 12 mm opening from vase mode; tape over it.
10. Belly: the aircraft lands on the LW-PLA belly. Glue a strip of 5–10 mm EVA foam along
    it (x ≈ 250–500) and land on grass.

### 4. Tail

The motor mount is a closed clamp and the tail block caps the boom, so both go on over the
boom's aft end, mount first. Nothing is threaded through the boom's holes until both are on;
anything sticking out of a hole would be pinched under their bores.

1. Cut a hatch in the lower surface of each V-tail panel about 55 mm aft of the LE and 30–60 mm
   from the root. Glue a `servo_cage` inside with the arm through a slot.
2. Epoxy a 4 mm carbon pin 12 mm into each `vtail_root_rib`. Slide the panels over the rib
   spigots and fit the `vtail_tip_cap`s.
3. Rear motor: screw the motor onto `rear_motor_mount` first. The plate stands 25 mm above the
   clamp, so a 2.5 mm L-key fits under the screw heads. Slide the mount onto the boom from the
   aft end, with the motor shaft at x = 820 and the plate level. Tighten the 2× M3×16 clamp
   bolts.
4. Epoxy the `tail_block` onto the aft end with its wire holes over the boom holes. Epoxy each
   root rib to its angled face with the pin in the 8 mm hole. Sight from behind that the V is
   symmetric to the wing, then leave it until the epoxy has cured.
5. Leads: push a fish tape (or a stiff wire with a bent tip) in through each lead hole and
   forward along the boom, until it comes out of the boom's front end inside `fuse_3`. Reach it
   through the nose opening. Tie on the lead's bare end and pull it through, then fit the
   connector at the front.
   - The rear motor's three phase leads (extended ~450 mm) go in at x ≈ 795. Keep them twisted
     together.
   - The two ruddervator leads go in through the tail block.
   - The rear ESC sits on the floor beside the end of the boom (x ≈ 400).
6. Ruddervators: tape hinge on top, horns underneath, 1.2 mm pushrods.

### 5. Electronics

- Pixhawk on the `fc_shelf` on foam tape, arrow forward. Power module and receiver in
  `fuse_2`; airspeed sensor near x = 400 with the pitot in the right wing LE at y ≈ 400
  (outside the prop disc).
- GPS/compass on top inside `fuse_3` near x = 560, aft of the ESCs and battery leads. The rear
  motor's phase leads run in the boom about 70 mm below it; keep the three twisted together so
  their fields cancel. Twist every power lead and run it along the floor. Check the compass for
  motor interference in the first hover log.
- Front ESCs in the root sections between the spars (x ≈ 440–475), connectors at the wing
  root. The rear ESC sits at the front of `fuse_3` (x ≈ 400) by the end of the boom. **No ESC
  may touch the LW-PLA skin**, which softens at 55–60 °C. Heat-shrink them and tie them to the
  spars or the boom with an air gap all round.
- A 5–6 V, ≥5 A BEC powers the servo rail (the Pixhawk does not). Heat-shrink or braid
  every wire where it crosses carbon.

### 6. Final assembly and balance

1. Slide the joiners through the fuselage. Plug both wings on and pin each rear spar to the rear
   joiner with the M2.5 nylon pin.
2. Tilt both motors fully forward and balance on your fingertips **431 mm aft of the nose tip**.
   The estimate puts the battery centre at x = 284 mm; anywhere in 270–305 mm keeps the CG in
   the 25–30 % band. The 3 mm stop pin in the tray's last hole (x = 360) holds the battery at
   281 mm (CG 430 mm). Use a hole further forward if the real CG comes out aft.
3. If it will not balance in that range, move equipment. Do not add dead weight at the tail.
4. Then set up PX4: `PX4_GUIDE.md`.

## Rules for the whole build

- Ream printed holes to size with a hand-held drill bit: spars 10.25 / 6.25 mm, boom 16.3 mm,
  joiner sleeves 8.3 / 4.3 mm, bearing pocket 8.1 mm. Never force a part; PETG cracks.
- Cut the 8 mm brim off the LW-PLA skins with a sharp knife. Peeling it tears the 0.5 mm wall.
- Carbon tubes of the same nominal size do not always telescope. Dry-fit the joiners in the
  spars and sand them lightly until they slide by hand.
- Light-coloured LW-PLA only. Never leave the plane in the sun or a hot car.
- Weigh every part as it comes off the bed (`tracker.md`), and re-run `mass_balance.py` with
  the real masses before balancing.

## Open questions, to confirm in flight test

- Real LW-PLA flow and wall thickness, from the test print.
- CG and trim: balance, then glide-test before any powered transition.
- Transition schedule and airspeeds: start from `PX4_GUIDE.md`, tune in the field.
- Vibration on the printed yokes and boom: check the logs after the first hover.
- Belly durability and compass interference: check after the first flights.
