# Custom VTOL RC Plane — Design and Build Guide (v0.2)

Every part is generated by the scripts in `scripts/`; `scripts/params.py` is the single source
of truth for dimensions. Numbers below come from `scripts/mass_balance.py` (masses from the
sliced G-code) and are checked by `scripts/check_parts.py`. If this file and the scripts ever
disagree, the scripts win.

## Configuration

- **Layout:** tilt-rotor tricopter, the same architecture as the Flightory Stallion: two front
  motors on tilting yokes ahead of the wing, one fixed lift motor on the tail boom, V-tail.
  Yaw in hover is differential tilt of the front motors.
- **Flight controller:** Pixhawk 6C, PX4 tilt-rotor VTOL (`PX4_GUIDE.md`). ArduPilot is the
  fallback; the hardware is the same.
- **Manual link:** ELRS receiver. Phone-over-4G MAVLink can be added later.

## Coordinates used in this guide

x is measured aft from the nose tip, y toward the right wing tip, z up from the fuselage
centreline. The wing's flat bottom is at z = −5 mm. The left side is the mirror image of the
right; every left-hand part has its own file.

## How it is built

- **Skins (LW-PLA, vase mode):** wing sections, ailerons, fuselage sections, V-tail panels and
  ruddervators are single-wall shells, open at both ends. Vase mode cannot print holes, so
  every hole in a skin is drilled by hand (table below). The root and mid wing sections also
  carry internal spanwise webs, printed as part of the same single wall ("Inside the wing").
  The wing and V-tail tips are closed by vase-mode tip fairings ("Tips").
- **Structure (PETG):** every skin end slides 10 mm over a PETG spigot ring (wing ribs, boom
  cup, couplers, V-tail ribs, the hidden tip rings). The rings are 0.85 mm inside the skin
  surface: 0.6 mm for the foamed wall plus 0.25 mm slip.
- **Wing:** two carbon spars per panel run from the root rib (y = 60) to the tip ring (y = 710):
  main 10×8 mm at x = 432, z = 3.5; rear 6×4 mm at x = 480, z = 1. The panels plug onto
  joiners that cross the fuselage in two printed frames: an 8×6 mm tube 420 mm long inside
  the main spars, and a 4 mm rod 320 mm long inside the rear spars. An M2.5 nylon pin locks
  each rear spar to the rear joiner.
- **Tilt mechanism:** a 6 mm boom rib sits between the root and mid sections at y = ±280. Its
  tongue runs forward into a PETG pod that holds the tilt servo. The yoke carries the motor
  and straddles the pod. Its inboard arm is screwed to the servo horn (direct drive). Its
  outboard arm turns on a flanged bearing on the pod's axle boss. The tilt axis is at x = 315,
  65 mm ahead of the LE. Tilt range is −20° (back) to 90° (forward); 0° is thrust up.
- **Fuselage:** four sections joined by PETG couplers at x = 240, 370 and 610. The nose
  (`fuse_1`) comes off for battery access. A 16×14 mm carbon tail boom runs from x = 420 to
  1050 at z = −20. It passes through both frames, the boom web in `coupler_610` and the
  `pod_collar`. The rear motor mount clamps the boom at x = 780, 2 mm behind the fuselage's
  end. The tail block on the end of
  the boom carries the V-tail.

## Inside the wing: ribs, spars and webs

Each wing has three PETG ribs (root, boom, mid/tip) and a hidden tip ring, threaded on two
carbon spars. The
skins between them are 204–217 mm long and need support from inside:

- **The spars carry the bending.** At 3 g the main spars see about 62 MPa, well within carbon's
  range.
- **The skin is the weak point.** At the root its top surface is strained to about 0.44, 0.87
  and 1.31 × 10⁻³ at 1, 2 and 3 g. A wide, gently curved 0.5 mm panel wrinkles at about
  0.5–0.9 × 10⁻³. In hover the root bends the other way and puts the flat bottom in
  compression. So the root and mid sections carry **spanwise webs** from the bottom skin to
  the top skin:
  - root: x = 405, 438.7 (behind the main spar), 475.4 and 484.7 (either side of the rear
    spar), 515 and 545, so no panel is wider than 37 mm. The wire bundle runs in the bay
    between 438.7 and 475.4;
  - mid: x = 402, 438.7, 484.7 and 505. The aileron servo's bay between 438.7 and 484.7 is
    46 mm wide: a web ahead of the rear spar would run through the hatch opening. The mid
    section's top skin sees about a third of the root's strain (0.17 × 10⁻³ at 1 g at its
    inboard end), so this panel reaches the wrinkling strain of a flat 46 mm panel only near
    3 g, where the root's 37 mm panels are already well past theirs;
  - tip: none. Its top skin sees about 0.25 × 10⁻³ at 3 g, a fifth of what wrinkles it.
- **How the webs print.** Each web is a 0.06 mm slot cut up from the bottom surface to 0.6 mm
  under the top surface. In vase mode the single wall runs up one side of the slot and back
  down the other, so the web prints as a fused double wall joined to the top skin, with no
  extra start or travel move. Webs ramp in at under 45° and stop 12 mm from each section end,
  where the spigots slide in. Both vase profiles need `slice_closing_radius` 0.01 and
  `detect_thin_wall` 0 for this (`SLICING.md`). `check_parts.py` checks in the G-code that
  every web is fused to the skin.
- **Clearances.** The webs stay 1.2 mm off the spars along the whole span and leave the
  wire/pitot line (x = 457) clear in every section. The motor leads from the boom cup cross the
  main spar in the last 12 mm of the root section, where the webs have run out. Around the
  pitot (y = 392–408) the webs ahead of the wire line are left open.
- **Cost.** The webs add about 30 g: a root skin goes from 23.8 to 33.5 g and a mid skin from
  15.8 to 20.7 g.
- **V-tail and fuselage need nothing more.** The V-tail skin is about 12 times stiffer in
  bending than a 4 mm spar would be, and its strain at CL 0.5 and 25 m/s (about 0.56 × 10⁻³) is
  fine for normal flight. The fuselage shell has a 4–15× margin against buckling. Never lift the
  plane by the V-tail.

## Access and openings

The fuselage is closed except at the nose; the wing and V-tail have a screwed hatch under every
servo:

- **The nose (`fuse_1`) is the fuselage's only hatch.** One M3 nylon screw holds it. With it off there is a
  115 × 135 mm opening at x = 220–240, and everything that needs service is within reach:
  - the battery slides out forward on its tray;
  - the Pixhawk (x 263–347) and the boards under the shelf are 25–110 mm in;
  - the wing plugs (x ≈ 330) are about 90 mm in;
  - the ESC block (x 360–410) and the boom's front end (x 420) are 120–180 mm in, above the
    battery's aft end. Take the battery out first.
- **Servo hatches:** each aileron and ruddervator servo is screwed to a `servo_hatch` that sits
  flush in its skin, held by two M2 screws. Nothing that needs service is glued in.
- **Tilt servos:** in the pods ahead of the wing, between each yoke's arms; their plugs sit in a
  pocket under each servo, open on the pod's inboard face.
- **Sealed for good:** everything aft of x ≈ 450 once `fuse_4` is on (step 3.7). Nothing lives
  there, and the GPS cable is laid in during step 3.
- **Outside:** the GPS sits on its saddle on top of `fuse_3` (x = 560), so it never has to be
  reached from inside. The pitot sticks out of the right wing's LE.
- **Taking a wing off:** remove the nose. Unplug that wing's three motor bullets and three
  servo plugs at x ≈ 330, and the pitot tubing on the right wing. Pull the M2.5 lock pin, then
  slide the wing off the joiners, feeding its leads out through the Ø12 hole.
- **Cooling air:** the 12 mm hole that vase mode leaves at the nose tip is the inlet; leave it
  open. Two Ø10 outlets in the belly behind the ESC block (x = 445, y = ±28) let the air out
  past the battery and the ESCs.

## Servicing: battery, servos and motors

| Job | How | Tools, time |
|---|---|---|
| **Battery out** | Undo the nose screw under the nose (x = 228) and slide the nose forward off its coupler. Open the two Velcro straps (x = 280 and 330), unplug the XT60 just inside the opening, and slide the pack forward out through the coupler ring (10 mm clear each side). In: slide it back until it touches the stop pin, strap it, plug in, nose on. | small screwdriver (none with a nylon thumb screw); 1 min |
| **Aileron or ruddervator servo** | Unclip the pushrod's clevis from the horn. Undo the hatch's two M2 screws; the hatch comes out with the servo on it, hanging on its lead. Pull the plug joint out of the opening and unplug it. Undo the two tab screws and fit the new servo, arm through the cover's slot. | small Phillips; 5 min |
| **Tilt servo** | Undo the two horn screws in the yoke's inboard arm and the axle screw on the outboard side. Lift the yoke off the pod, away from the tilt axis; it stays tethered by the motor's flex loop until the leads are separated. Take the horn's centre screw out, undo the two tab screws and slide the servo out inboard; its lead lifts out of the pocket under it with the plug, which unplugs there. Refit in the order of steps 2.4–2.6, drilling the new disc horn through the arm's existing holes. | small Phillips, 2.5 mm hex; 15 min |
| **Front motor** | Lift the yoke off as for the tilt servo, separate the three leads at their joints in the flex loop (unplug, or cut and re-solder) and undo the four M3×8 screws under the yoke plate. | 2.5 mm hex, soldering iron unless bulleted; 15 min |
| **Rear motor** | Separate its three leads at their joints between the motor and the boom's lead hole (x ≈ 803, just behind the clamp), and undo its four M3×8 screws from under the plate; a 2.5 mm L-key fits there with the mount in place. | 2.5 mm L-key, soldering iron unless bulleted; 5 min |
| **Pixhawk, receiver, boards** | Nose off; all within 110 mm of the opening. Take the battery out first for anything under the shelf. | 5 min |
| **ESC block** | Battery out, then reach 120–180 mm in over the tray, undo the block's two straps and lift it down. | 10 min |

The nose screw is the only thing between you and the battery. Keep a spare M3 nylon screw in
the field box: if it is lost the nose can depart in flight, and the CG jumps aft.

## Before printing the full set

1. **Measure the tilt servos.** The pod bay fits a 24 × 13 mm body, 21 mm deep. The tab holes
   are 28 mm apart, the output spline is 6 mm from the front of the body, and the tab face is
   ~11 mm below the top of the spline. If yours differ, change `SERVO_*` in `params.py` and run
   `build_all.py` before printing the pods and yokes.
2. **Test-fit a servo hatch:** PETG plate `petg_1_fit_tests` carries one `servo_hatch` and two
   `hatch_block`s (and the tip ring for step 4). Your 9 g servo's
   tabs must screw to the posts (holes 28 mm apart) with its body (up to 23 × 12.2 mm) between
   them, and the horn must stay within 15 mm of the cover's centre, short of the ledges.
3. **LW-PLA test:** print `wing_tip_r` on the X, which prints every LW-PLA part. Check two
   things: it should weigh 14.7 g ±15 %, and its wall must be closed and even (no gaps between
   the layers, no light through it). Then flex it gently.
   - Weight off: the extruder feeds the wrong amount. Change the flow in `filament_lwpla.json`
     and re-slice.
   - Weight right but the wall gappy: the filament foams less than the profile expects. Raise
     the flow a little at a time.
   - About 27 % light (~10.7 g): the spool is pre-foamed (`BOM.md`, "Filament"). Do not raise
     the flow to make the weight: the part would weigh right with about a quarter of the plastic
     missing from its walls. These files are for active-foaming LW-PLA only.

   Every vase part prints at 17–30 mm/s, so this one test covers them all.
4. **Fit test:** slide `rib_tip_r` (the tip ring, from the same plate) into that tip skin. It should go in with
   light hand force. If it is tight, sand the ring; never force a skin. Then print
   `tip_fairing_r` and slide it onto the ring's outboard half: it must meet the skin's end edge
   to edge. Its top closes as a thin fused fin; if the top loops sag instead, lengthen
   `WING_TIP_CLOSE` and `VT_TIP_CLOSE` in `scripts/params.py` (less lean) and rebuild.
5. **Web check:** print `wing_mid_r` and look into its open ends. Every web must run from the
   bottom skin to the top skin and be joined to it ("Inside the wing").
6. Then print the rest (`tracker.md`), both printers at once: the X prints every LW-PLA skin
   (about 49 h), the S1 the PETG as seven plates (about 16 h). Nothing needs supports. Hold
   PETG plates 6 and 7 until the 3S changes are built in (`PLAN_3S.md`): they carry the motor
   mounts and the FC shelf.

## Assembly

Dry-fit every step before mixing glue. Use epoxy for every carbon bond (sand the carbon first)
and every structural PETG joint. Thin epoxy or medium CA is fine for skin-to-spigot joints.

### 1. Wing panels (each side)

1. Ream the spar holes in every rib and the boom cup to 10.25 mm / 6.25 mm with a hand-held
   drill bit, until both spars slide through all of them.
2. Epoxy the `boom_cup` to the inboard face of the `boom_rib`. Thread both spars (wrapped in
   tape, so they do not get glued) through the cup and the rib to align them. The cup's plate
   closes the rib's wire groove.
3. Aileron servo hatches (4 per aircraft: `wing_mid` at y ≈ 325, `wing_tip` at y ≈ 530).
   Before the panel is threaded, with the skin loose:
   1. Lay a `servo_hatch` on the bottom skin, centred at x = 458 (between the main spar's aft
      web at 438.7 and the rear spar), long side spanwise. Draw round it and cut the opening
      0.2 mm outside the line. The opening stays 1.7 mm clear of the web; never cut into a web.
      The tip skin has no webs.
   2. Put a strip of tape over both ends of the cover's inner face, so the epoxy cannot glue
      the hatch in. Butter the outer (glue) faces of two `hatch_block`s with epoxy and put
      them in through the opening. Set the hatch in the opening and drive an M2×6 screw through
      each end of the cover into its block: the ledges pull up under the cover's ends and the
      blocks' glue faces seat on the inside of the skin. Tape the cover flush from outside and
      let it cure, then take the hatch out: the blocks stay.
   3. Screw the servo to the hatch's posts (tabs flat on the posts, 28 mm apart; the posts fit
      a body up to 23 mm long) and cut a slot in the cover for the arm.
   4. Lay the servo's lead and its extension (500 mm mid, 800 mm tip) to the wire line at
      x = 457, over the servo, with the plug joint in the bay beside the servo and ~60 mm of
      slack, so the hatch comes out far enough to unplug it.
4. Thread the panel onto the spars from the root: `rib_root`, `wing_root`, boom rib with
   cup, `wing_mid`, `rib_midtip`, `wing_tip`, then the tip ring `rib_tip` (its full-chord side
   outboard). Feed the aileron leads (and, on the
   right wing, the pitot tubing) through the Ø10–12 holes at x = 457 in every rib. Epoxy
   the spars into the rib bosses and the skins onto the spigots. Keep the panel flat on the
   table and sight along the LE for twist before the glue sets.
   Each spigot leaves 0.25 mm of slip per side. A skin pushed to one side therefore shows a
   step of up to 0.25 mm at its rib. Before the glue sets, run a fingertip over every seam
   and centre the skin until it is flush with the rib all the way round.
   Then slide the `tip_fairing` over the tip ring until it butts against the end of `wing_tip`,
   line the two up flush all round and wick thin CA into the seam. There is no rib band here:
   the two skins meet edge to edge, as the fuselage sections do.
5. Ailerons: tape hinge along the top surface. They deflect ±25°: the bevel under the
   aileron's front face clears the wing. Glue a `control_horn` under each aileron in line
   with its servo arm. Fit a 1.2 mm pushrod with a Z-bend in the servo arm and a snap clevis
   (or EZ connector) at the horn: it sets the neutral, and unhooks so the hatch can come out.
6. Lock pin: drill Ø2.5 straight down through the wing at x = 480, y = ±68, using the root
   rib's vertical guide hole. It goes through the skin, the rib ring and the rear spar. The rear
   joiner gets the matching hole at final assembly.

### 2. Tilt mechanism (each side)

The yoke cannot be slid sideways onto the pod: its arms clear the pod and the axle boss by
0.5 mm, far too little to push the horn onto the servo's spline. So the horn goes on the spline
first, the yoke goes on from above, and the horn screws go in last, through the yoke's inboard
arm.

1. Press an F693ZZ flanged bearing into the yoke's outboard arm. Melt an M3 heat-set insert
   into the pod's axle boss.
2. **Screw the motor onto the yoke now**: 4× M3×8. With the yoke on the pod the screw heads
   can't be reached. Check by hand that no screw touches the windings. Join the motor's leads
   to their extensions ~40 mm from the motor (solder and heat-shrink, or 3.5 mm bullets): the
   joints stay outside the pod, so the motor can be changed with the yoke off.
3. Epoxy the pod onto the boom-rib tongue and bolt it with 2× M3×35 + nyloc nuts (the pod is
   28 mm wide). The 6 mm tongue goes into a 6.3 mm slot; if the first-layer squish makes it
   tight, sand the tongue. The motor wires run in the groove under the tongue, through the cup
   into the root section.
4. Tilt servo: lay a 600 mm extension lead from the pod's plug pocket (under the servo bay,
   open on the inboard face) through the wire channel and the tongue groove to the wing's wire
   line. Fit the servo in the bay, tabs on the pod's inboard face (2 screws). Its lead drops
   into the pocket; plug it into the extension there and push the joint into the pocket.
   Power the servo at its centre position and press a round (disc) horn on its spline. Fit
   the horn's centre screw.
5. Hold the yoke at 35° (the middle of −20…90°) and lower it over the pod from above, arms
   either side of it, outboard arm's bearing onto the axle boss. On the outboard side fit the
   **0.5 mm shim** (OD ≤ 5 mm) between the bearing's inner race and the boss, then the M3×12
   screw and washer. The shim fills the 0.5 mm running gap. Without it, tightening the screw
   pulls the yoke against the pod and it binds.
6. With the servo still at centre and the yoke at 35°, drill two Ø1.5 holes through the
   yoke's inboard arm into the disc horn, 7 mm either side of the axis, with tape on the bit
   6.5 mm from its tip (arm 4 mm + horn 2.5 mm: the servo case is behind it). Fit two M2×6
   self-tapping screws. The Ø7 hole in the arm on the axis gives a screwdriver the horn's
   centre screw later. The yoke must swing freely from −20° to 90° with the axle screw tight.
7. Leave a loop in the motor wires at the pod's outboard wire hole, with the joints in it, so
   they flex through the full tilt range.

### 3. Fuselage and tail boom

1. Ream every boom hole to 16.3 mm while the parts are still loose: `frame_main`, `frame_rear`,
   the web of `coupler_610`, `pod_collar`, `rear_motor_mount` and `tail_block`. The boom must
   slide through each one by hand.
2. Mark and drill both sides of `fuse_3`: main joiner Ø8.5 at x = 432, z = +3.5; rear joiner
   Ø4.5 at x = 480, z = +1; wing wiring Ø12 at x = 457, z = +7. On the top centreline drill
   Ø16 at x = 560 for the GPS cable (it passes the 10-pin JST-GH plug). In the belly drill two
   Ø10 cooling-air outlets at x = 445, y = ±28.
3. Slide `frame_main` and `frame_rear` into `fuse_3`. Push the joiners through the skin holes
   and the frame sleeves to align them, then epoxy the frames to the skin. Epoxy the
   `gps_mount` onto the top of `fuse_3`, centred over the Ø16 hole and square to the
   centreline. Then lay the GPS cable, or a pull cord for it, from that hole forward along the
   top of `fuse_3`, through the frames' upper openings and out of its front end. After step 7
   nothing aft of x ≈ 450 can be reached.
4. Epoxy `coupler_370` between `fuse_2` and `fuse_3`. Epoxy `coupler_610` into the aft end of
   `fuse_3` with its boom web forward, inside `fuse_3`. As with the wing skins, the couplers
   leave 0.25 mm of slip per side: line `fuse_2` and `fuse_3` up all the way round before the
   glue sets.
5. Prepare the boom. Drill Ø6 at x ≈ 803 for the rear motor leads, on the side that will face
   up (5 mm behind the motor mount's clamp, which sits at x = 762–798). Dry-fit the
   `tail_block` on the aft end, square to that hole, and mark the boom through
   the block's two wire holes (x ≈ 1001 right, 1024 left). Take the block off and drill Ø6 there.
6. Slide the boom into `fuse_3` through its open aft end, front end first. It passes the
   `coupler_610` web, `frame_rear` and `frame_main`; stop when its front end is at x = 420,
   with the lead hole facing up. Epoxy it at all three, reaching in from the open aft end.
7. Epoxy the `pod_collar` into the tail end of `fuse_4`. Slide `fuse_4` over the boom's aft end,
   through the collar and onto the aft half of `coupler_610`. Epoxy it to the coupler and the
   collar to the boom, lining `fuse_4` up with `fuse_3` all the way round before the glue sets.
   Nothing aft of x ≈ 450 can be reached afterwards, and nothing lives in
   `fuse_4`. The boom's aft end stays free for the motor mount and tail block.
8. Melt the M3 insert into `coupler_240` and epoxy the coupler 20 mm into the front of
   `fuse_2`. **Thread the two battery straps through the `battery_tray` first**: each goes down
   one slot beside the battery's place (x = 280 and 330), under the plate through the gaps in
   both rails, and up the other slot. They cannot be threaded once the tray is in. Then slide
   the `fc_shelf` and the tray in through the front and epoxy them in; both reach past the
   joint at x = 370 into `fuse_3`.
9. Slide on the nose. Drill Ø3.4 through its bottom centreline at x = 228 (12 mm ahead of its
   aft edge) into the insert, and fit the M3×12 nylon screw with a 12 mm nylon washer. Leave
   the 12 mm opening that vase mode leaves at the nose tip open: it is the cooling-air inlet.
   A scrap of nylon mesh glued inside keeps grass out.
10. Belly: the aircraft lands on the LW-PLA belly. Glue a strip of 5–10 mm EVA foam along
    it (x ≈ 250–500) and land on grass.

### 4. Tail

The motor mount is a closed clamp and the tail block caps the boom, so both go on over the
boom's aft end, mount first. Nothing is threaded through the boom's holes until both are on;
anything sticking out of a hole would be pinched under their bores.

1. Ruddervator servo hatches, before the panels go on: on each panel's lower surface, centre a
   `servo_hatch` 55 mm aft of the LE and 45 mm out from the panel's root edge, long side
   spanwise. Cut the opening and fit the hatch blocks and the servo exactly as for the wing
   (step 1.3). The flat cover sits flush with the curved skin along its front and back edges
   and up to 0.45 mm under it along the middle, and the blocks' glue faces stand 0.3–0.45 mm
   off the skin: use thickened epoxy there. Lay a 650 mm extension from the servo to the root,
   joint beside the servo with ~60 mm of slack.
2. Epoxy a 4 mm carbon pin 12 mm into each `vtail_root_rib`. Slide the panels over the rib
   spigots. Glue a `vtail_tip_cap` ring 6 mm into each panel's tip, its full-chord side out,
   then slide a `vtail_tip_fairing` over it until it butts against the panel. The spigots have
   the same 0.25 mm slip per side as the wing: line each panel up with its rib and its fairing
   all the way round before the glue sets.
3. Rear motor: screw the motor onto `rear_motor_mount` first. The plate stands 25 mm above the
   clamp, so a 2.5 mm L-key fits under the screw heads. Slide the mount onto the boom from the
   aft end, with the motor shaft at x = 780 and the plate level. Tighten the 2× M3×16 clamp
   bolts.
4. Epoxy the `tail_block` onto the aft end with its wire holes over the boom holes. Epoxy each
   root rib to its angled face with the pin in the 8 mm hole. Sight from behind that the V is
   symmetric to the wing, then leave it until the epoxy has cured.
5. Leads: push a fish tape (or a stiff wire with a bent tip) in through each lead hole and
   forward along the boom, until it comes out of the boom's front end inside `fuse_3`. Reach it
   through the nose opening. Tie on the lead's bare end and pull it through, then fit the
   connector at the front.
   - The rear motor's three phase leads (extended ~450 mm) go in at x ≈ 803, behind the clamp:
     run them down the mount's aft face to the hole. Keep them twisted
     together, and make their joints to the motor's own leads outside the boom, between the
     motor and the hole, where they can be reached to change the motor.
   - The two ruddervator leads go in through the tail block.
   - All of them come out of the boom's front end at x = 420, right behind the ESC block.
6. Ruddervators: tape hinge on top, horns underneath, 1.2 mm pushrods with a Z-bend in the
   servo arm and a clevis at the horn.

### 5. Electronics

Where each item sits comes from `scripts/payload.py`; the gate checks that it all fits and
clears the printed parts. `renders/29–33` show it inside the fuselage.

- **Pixhawk** on the front of the `fc_shelf` (x 263–347) on 3 mm foam, arrow forward.
- **Under the shelf**, above the battery, on Velcro: power module and servo BEC on the left,
  receiver and airspeed sensor on the right. The pitot sits in the right wing LE at y ≈ 400
  (outside the prop disc; at y ≈ 505 for the 3S build's bigger props, "Holes and cuts made by
  hand"); its tubing follows the wire hole into the fuselage.
- **ESC block:** heat-shrink the three ESCs, stand them on edge side by side with 3 mm foam
  between them, and tape them into one 42 × 50 × 28 mm block. Hang it under the shelf's aft
  part (x 360–410) on Velcro, with two 20 mm straps that pass down through the shelf's side
  openings, under the block and back over the shelf. It sits 16 mm above the battery and more
  than 10 mm from the skin. **No ESC may touch the LW-PLA skin**, which softens at 55–60 °C.
  The rear motor's leads come out of the boom's front end right behind the block.
- **Wing plugs:** each wing's three motor leads and three servo leads (two ailerons, tilt servo)
  come into the fuselage through the Ø12 hole at x = 457 and end in plugs at x ≈ 330: 3.5 mm
  bullets for the motor, servo plugs for the rest. They are reachable through the nose, so a
  wing comes off without opening anything else. Short pigtails run on from the plugs to the
  ESC block and the Pixhawk. The servos' own plugs meet their extensions where a hatch or the
  pod's plug pocket can reach them, never deep inside a wing.
- **Motor-lead joints** (motor lead to extension) sit outside the pod, in the flex loop, and
  outside the boom, between the rear motor and its lead hole. Solder and heat-shrink them, or
  use 3.5 mm bullets there (~7 g more, which costs ~0.25 % of the front motors' hover margin):
  either way a motor can be changed without opening anything.
- **GPS/compass** on its saddle on top of `fuse_3` at x = 560, arrow forward, on foam tape. Its
  cable runs down through the Ø16 hole and forward along the top, laid in during step 3.3. Use
  its compass and check it for motor interference in the first hover log (`PX4_GUIDE.md`).
  Twist every power lead and keep the three phase leads of each motor together, so their
  fields cancel.
- A 5–6 V, ≥5 A BEC powers the servo rail (the Pixhawk does not). Heat-shrink or braid every
  wire where it crosses carbon.
- **Cooling:** air comes in at the nose tip and leaves through the two belly outlets behind the
  ESC block. Keep the path clear: do not stuff foam around the battery.

### 6. Final assembly and balance

1. Slide the joiners through the fuselage. Plug both wings on and pin each rear spar to the rear
   joiner with the M2.5 nylon pin.
2. Tilt both motors fully forward and balance on your fingertips **433 mm aft of the nose tip**.
   The estimate puts the battery centre at x = 295 mm; anywhere in 271–306 mm keeps the CG in
   the 25–30 % band. The 3 mm stop pin goes in the aftmost tray hole that keeps the CG within
   29.5 % MAC: the hole at x = 380, which holds the battery at 301 mm (CG 435 mm, 29.3 %). The
   holes run from x = 250 to 390: move the pin aft if the real CG comes out
   forward, forward if it comes out aft. The hover margin wants the CG as far aft as the band
   allows: each hole forward puts ~0.5 % more of the hover load on the front motors, and at
   x = 360 they would already need more than 60 % of their thrust.
3. If it will not balance in that range, move equipment. Do not add dead weight at the tail.
4. Then set up PX4: `PX4_GUIDE.md`.

## Rules for the whole build

- Ream printed holes to size with a hand-held drill bit: spars 10.25 / 6.25 mm, boom 16.3 mm,
  joiner sleeves 8.3 / 4.3 mm, bearing pocket 8.1 mm. Never force a part; PETG cracks.
- Cut the 8 mm brim off the LW-PLA skins with a sharp knife. Peeling it tears the 0.5 mm wall.
- Carbon tubes of the same nominal size do not always telescope. Dry-fit the joiners in the
  spars and sand them lightly until they slide by hand.
- Light-coloured LW-PLA only. Never leave the plane in the sun or a hot car.
- Weigh every part as it comes off the bed (`tracker.md`), and re-run `mass_balance.py` with
  the real masses before balancing.

## Finish: making the glued wing read as one piece

Every rib flange is ruled between the skin sections at its two faces, so it continues the
skin's taper with no crease. At every seam but one it also uses the skin's own points. The
exception is the boom rib's outboard face, where the full-chord rib meets the hinge-cut mid
skin; the shapes there still agree within 0.02 mm. The fuselage sections, and each tip skin
and its tip fairing, meet edge to edge over a hidden ring.
Two gate checks hold this. "Glued skin joints are flush" keeps each seam within 0.15 mm, and
the model measures 0.04 mm or less. "Rib flanges and tip fairings keep the skin taper"
catches a flange or fairing that would leave a crease. In `renders/`, 12–15 and 17–20 show the
wing and every joint in one colour, and 22–25 show them with reflection stripes. The stripes
run straight across every seam. What still shows once glued:

- **PETG rib bands**, 3 mm wide (6 mm at the boom rib), if the PETG is a different colour
  from the LW-PLA. Print the PETG in the same colour, or paint. The tips have none.
- **Glue lines.** Wipe off squeeze-out before it cures.
- **The pod fairing.** Ahead of x ≈ 422 the boom rib's tongue rises up to 18 mm above the
  wing (and 5 mm below the LE) into the pod. It is part of the pod mount, not a step.
- **Control-surface gaps,** including where nothing sits behind the hinge line: y 283–286
  and 485–492 on the wing. At each tip the aileron (and the ruddervator) stops 2 mm short of
  the tip fairing.

For a painted finish, sand the seams lightly (400 grit; the LW-PLA wall is only ~0.5 mm), then
apply a thin filler primer and a light-coloured paint. Paint adds weight: keep it to a light
coat and balance again afterwards.

## Tips

The wing and V-tail tips are closed by LW-PLA vase-mode fairings (`tip_fairing_l/r`,
`vtail_tip_fairing` ×2), so no flat end, rib band or spar end shows. Each butts against its
skin's end over a hidden PETG ring (`rib_tip`, `vtail_tip_cap`), the way the fuselage sections
join, and runs straight on for 6 mm before it closes:

- **Wing:** the flat bottom runs on flat and the upper surface curves down to meet it at a
  sharp edge 32 mm out from the skin (a Hoerner tip, which sheds the tip vortex a little further
  out than a square tip does). The leading edge pulls back 18 mm and the trailing edge in 8 mm,
  rounding both corners of the planform. The fairing is full chord, so it also closes the
  space outboard of the aileron's end; the tip ring is solid behind the hinge there.
- **V-tail:** both faces close symmetrically onto the chord plane 20 mm out, with the leading
  edge pulled back 9 mm and the trailing edge in 4 mm. One file prints both.
- **Printing:** each stands on its root and closes at the top. The surfaces curve away from
  the skin tangentially, then run in at no more than 41.4° from vertical (37.6° on the V-tail;
  the vase limit is 45°),
  so nothing needs support. The last few tenths of a millimetre close as a fused edge.
- **Span:** the fairings take the span from 1420 to 1478 mm. The mass balance still counts
  only the area to the tip rings, so its stall speeds are conservative.

## Open questions, to confirm in flight test

- Real LW-PLA flow and wall thickness, from the test print.
- CG and trim: balance, then glide-test before any powered transition.
- Transition schedule and airspeeds: start from `PX4_GUIDE.md`, flown in simulation
  (`SIMULATION.md`), and tune in the field. In the first transition, check that the airspeed
  passes `VT_ARSP_TRANS` (14 m/s) well before the 15 s timeout.
- Vibration on the printed yokes and boom: check the logs after the first hover.
- Belly durability and compass interference: check after the first flights.
- Temperature inside the fuselage: after the first long hover, feel the ESC block and the
  battery. If either is hot, enlarge the belly outlets.
